Why Royal Match

A puzzle game that earns like a casino and sits outside the ban.

Royal Match is a match-3 puzzle from the Istanbul studio Dream Games. You swap tiles, clear a goal and restore a castle. No cash goes in and nothing cashes out.

It is also one of the highest-earning mobile games in the world. That mix is why we picked it: a skill game whose money machine looks a lot like a gambling app's, and which kept trading in India while real-money games were banned.

  • $1.4BRevenue in 2024, up 56% on the year before (AppMagic estimate).
  • 6 in 10Downloads came from paid channels, far more than its older rival Candy Crush (Sensor Tower).

Third-party estimates, summarized in web search. Read them as direction, not audited figures.

Why it's worth studying

Three reasons to take it apart.

  • It beat the incumbentRoyal Match overtook Candy Crush Saga in monthly revenue and downloads in 2023. Passing a long-established leader with a similar game takes more than a good puzzle.
  • It's the cleanest comparisonSame pressure points as a casino app: limited resources, offers at the stop point, live events, a few big spenders. Just no wager. Change only the stake and see what else changes.
  • The law treats it differentlyIndia's 2025 ban hit real-money games and left Royal Match alone. Asking why shows where the legal line sits.

1 · The loop

Two pop-ups, one moment.

That moment is the first thing we pull apart. The easy answer to the India question is "puzzles are nicer." The real answer is less comfortable: the two industries lean on a lot of the same psychology, and what separates them is whether you put money on the outcome.

First, three assumptions that are off:

  • The ban came from ParliamentIndia's Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans real-money games whether they run on skill or chance. Older court rulings went the other way for skill games.
  • GST didn't cause itA 28% tax on the full value of bets hurt a lot. But taxing a business assumes it's legal. The ban made it illegal.
  • "Gambling app" means two thingsReal-money apps are what India banned. Chips and spins sold for in-app purchases are social casino, a separate thing.
The loop

Do something, get a result, hit a stop point, see an offer.

Royal Match feeds a finish line, the castle, even though the line keeps moving as new areas ship. A casino loop has no finish line, only a VIP ladder that rises the longer you stay.

So what

The stop point is the product. Both games are built so the free supply runs out where the store is waiting.

2 · Skill and luck

Skill makes you try again. Luck makes you hope.

When you lose a puzzle, you blame your move. I should've used the rocket first. That feels fixable, so you retry, and paying for extra moves feels like a sensible shortcut. Royal Match is widely reported to mix easy levels with deliberately hard ones, so you win most of the time but hit a wall now and then (an industry pattern, not a Dream Games disclosure).

Chance games work on different wiring:

  • Illusion of controlYou pressed the button, so it feels like you did something.
  • The near-missTwo cherries and a third just out of line feels like almost winning, even though it's pure chance.
  • Variable rewardsPayouts at unpredictable intervals are the hardest habit to break (cited from memory: Skinner, Reid 1986, Clark et al. 2009).

Poker sits in the middle: enough skill to believe you can beat it, enough luck to keep you hoping. That ambiguity is why Indian courts argued about it for decades.

So what

Skill gives you someone to blame. Chance gives you something to hope for. Both keep you playing.

3 · The money

Both games are scarcity machines. They sell you more when you run out.

Royal Match juggles lives, moves, coins and stars. Stars can only be earned by playing, so the long-term goal can't be bought. Coins buy extra moves and boosters, and coins can be bought. On the Continue? screen above, 35 extra moves cost 900 coins, and the Royal Pass is pitched right underneath as a way to get them free. Its rewards are mostly fixed: you know a chest is a few wins away.

A social casino has one currency, chips, and higher stakes unlock as you play, so the pressure to spend rises the longer you stay. Its rewards are random, with levels and VIP tiers bolted on to give a sense of progress.

  • Who pays: Royal MatchA very broad base buying small conveniences (coin packs, lives, a pass), plus a tail of big spenders. It is reported to run little or no in-game advertising.
  • Who pays: social casinoNarrow and deep. A small group of heavy spenders carries the business. One listed company reported about 1 in 10 of its players paying in 2022 (SciPlay).

The hooks overlap: a "Best value" label that anchors you to the bigger pack, countdown timers, and an offer that appears right at the stop point, when walking away feels like losing what you've put in.

So what

Same moment, different shape: Royal Match sells small things to many people, a casino sells a lot to a few.

4 · The players

Royal Match buys its audience. Casino apps bought attention another way.

Royal Match leans on paid advertising and celebrity creative: Simon Cowell, Amanda Holden, Olly Murs and Tom Felton have all fronted campaigns (Marketing Beat, Pocket Gamer Biz). Nobody publishes the budget.

Indian real-money apps bought attention through sport. Dream11 held the Team India jersey rights and moved to exit when the law passed in 2025 (Gulf News). Referral cash and welcome bonuses completed the playbook, and the law now bars it.

Both engineer a fast first win. Royal Match does it with very easy early levels. Casino apps tune early outcomes and hand out free chips, which is where design starts to become a regulatory question (our reading).

  • Keeping: Royal MatchA rolling calendar of short events, team play (quitting lets teammates down), and new castle areas.
  • Keeping: gambling appsStreak bonuses that reset if you miss a day, jackpots that keep a rare big win in view, and VIP status that lapses if you stop.

The home screen is a wall of timers. A progress bar with a "x2 30m" boost, a rank race, and eight event icons, most of them counting down from "2d 12h". The heart shows infinite lives for a limited time.

There is always something to come back for today, which is the point.

Screenshot: Royal Match home screen.

So what

Heavy ad spend only works if players stay. The benchmarks suggest the puzzle genre keeps them better.

5 · The law

So why did ₹ India ban one and not the other?

It comes down to the stake.

India's Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans offering, advertising or processing payments for "online money games," including services run from abroad (Majmudar & Partners, SCC Online). The stated reasons were addiction, financial harm and young players.

  • Oct 202328% GST starts applying to the full value of bets (CyberPeace)
  • Aug 2025Parliament passes the Act
  • SinceThe Supreme Court is hearing a challenge; we found no final judgment

Dates are approximate. Sources disagree on when enforcement began, so check the Gazette.

Why Royal Match is untouched: nobody stakes money and nobody wins money. You can spend on it, but that buys convenience and progress, it can't be cashed out, and it gives no chance at a payout. So it falls under ordinary consumer rules, not gambling law.

The gray zone is social casino. It sells chips with no cash-out, which on its face sits outside "online money game." But a pending petition targets apps that look like social games and behave like gambling, so expect scrutiny (our reading, not legal advice).

Regulators follow the stake, not the mechanism.

So what

The psychology is the same. The law only cares about the money.

What we learned

Four things to take from all this.

  • The stop point is the productLives, moves and chip balances aren't just rules. They're where the store lives.
  • There's always a dialLevel difficulty in Royal Match, payout rate and bet range in a casino. Both get tuned from live data.
  • Live ops keeps the loop openEvents, streaks and bonuses give you a reason to come back today.
  • Regulation tracks the stakeSkill-based play with no wager sits on much safer ground than anything that touches cash.

One uncomfortable bit: the patterns that make a game feel good are the ones critics call manipulative when pointed at vulnerable players. A good analysis names that tension instead of only counting the upside.
